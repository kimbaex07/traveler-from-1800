## The entry

> Imagine every library and post office in the world joined by one road no wider than a thread, so a letter you write at dawn is read across the sea before the ink dries.

## Why this line

The sentence explains the internet with only things a person from 1800 already knows: a library, a post office, a road, a letter, ink. It joins the two things the internet is for most people — reading what the world knows and sending word to people far away — into one picture, and the road "no wider than a thread" carries the wonder of it: so much passing along so little. A letter read across the sea "before the ink dries" says, without any modern word, that distance has stopped mattering.

Every word in the line predates 1800 — no "internet", no "computer", no "wire", no "instant" — so the traveler is never asked to picture a thing from a century he has not lived in.

## How to run

```sh
python3 main.py
```

`main.py` reads `entry.txt` beside it, checks it is one sentence of 1 to 200 characters, and writes its exact bytes to `$MAKEFUN_OUTPUT_DIR/entry.txt` (default `output/entry.txt`).
